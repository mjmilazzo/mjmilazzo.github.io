Research Interests#

I use gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) to measure the trace pollutants in our atmosphere to understand their effects on human health and tropospheric chemistry.

Characterizing Speciated Organics Emitted from Fires#

As wildfires extend into the Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI), more communities face increased fire risk. In my main PhD research project, I investigate the chemicals in the smoke emitted from burning structures. We flew a drone into the smoke plume of 8 burning accessory dwelling units (ADUs) that were fully furnished, and analyzed the samples for speciated gas- and particle-phase organics, as well as particle-phase metals. These experiments are the largest of their kind to-date.

I also collaborated with the UC Berkeley Fire Research Lab, where I measured emissions from burning individual materials in controlled conditions. These materials were directly sourced from the ADU construction, providing a unique opportunity to directly compare laboratory-scale and full-scale emissions.

Urban Air Quality#

The partitioning of intermediate- and semi-volatile organic compounds (I/SVOCs) has important implications in atmospheric chemistry, yet has remained challenging to measure well. The comprehensive thermal desorption aerosol gas chromatograph (cTAG) is a custom GC-MS with online derivatization and calibrations, built in a collaboration between the Goldstein Group and Aerosol Dynamics Inc. cTAG measures hourly concentrations of I/SVOCs in the gas- and particle-phase (C5 to C32 equivalent alkane volatility). I deployed cTAG at UC Berkeley’s campus measuring ambient urban air quality and identified tracers of cooking emissions, personal care products, and trace amounts of wildfire smoke.

I have participated in other ambient measurement campaigns, including URBAN-EC at UC Berkeley (lead: Erin Katz) and ENACT for my M.S. research.

Diesel Emissions Testing#

Emissions from mobile sources have decreased due to improved engine design and emissions control systems, but some sources still produce high emissions in certain conditions. I am interested in understanding the conditions that produce high emissions in diesel engines, both on-road (e.g., trucks) and off-road (construction equipment, etc.). Emissions can be tested under controlled conditions on a chassis dynamometer (picture, left) or as used in the real world with a Portable Emissions Measurement System (PEMS).
