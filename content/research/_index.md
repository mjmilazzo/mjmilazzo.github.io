# Research Interests

I use gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) to measure the trace pollutants in our atmosphere to understand their effects on human health and tropospheric chemistry. 


## Characterizing Speciated Organics Emitted from Fires 

{{< img src="ADU.png" class="figure-left" width="30%" >}}

As wildfires extend into the Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI), more communities face increased fire risk. 
In my main PhD research project, I investigate the chemicals in the smoke emitted from burning structures. 
We flew a drone into the smoke plume of 8 burning accessory dwelling units (ADUs) that were fully furnished, and analyzed the samples for 
speciated gas- and particle-phase organics, as well as particle-phase metals. These experiments are the largest of their kind to-date.

{{< clear >}}

{{< img src="tube_heater.jpg" class="figure-left" width="30%" >}}

I also collaborated with the [UC Berkeley Fire Research Lab](https://firelab.berkeley.edu/), where I measured emissions from burning individual
materials in controlled conditions. These materials were directly sourced from the ADU construction, providing a unique opportunity to directly compare 
laboratory-scale and full-scale emissions.

## Urban Air Quality

{{< img src="cTAG.jpg" class="figure-left" width="30%" >}}

The partitioning of intermediate- and semi-volatile organic compounds (I/SVOCs) has important implications in atmospheric chemistry, yet has remained challenging to measure well.
The **comprehensive thermal desorption aerosol gas chromatograph (cTAG)** is a custom GC-MS with online derivatization and calibrations, 
built in a collaboration between the Goldstein Group and [Aerosol Dynamics Inc](https://aerosol.us/). 
cTAG measures hourly concentrations of I/SVOCs in the gas- and particle-phase (C~5~ to C~32~ equivalent alkane volatility). 
I deployed cTAG at UC Berkeley's campus measuring ambient urban air quality and identified tracers of 
cooking emissions, personal care products, and trace amounts of wildfire smoke.

I have participated in other ambient measurement campaigns, including [URBAN-EC](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/25/15281/2025/) at UC Berkeley (lead: Erin Katz)
and [ENACT](https://www.enactalabama.org/) for my M.S. research.

## Diesel Emissions Testing

{{< img src="dyno.jpg" class="figure-left" width="30%" >}}

Emissions from mobile sources have decreased due to improved engine design and emissions control systems, but some sources still produce high emissions in certain conditions.
I am interested in understanding the conditions that produce high emissions in diesel engines, both on-road (e.g., trucks) and off-road (construction equipment, etc.). 
Emissions can be tested under controlled conditions on a chassis dynamometer (picture, left) or as used in the real world with a Portable Emissions Measurement System (PEMS).
